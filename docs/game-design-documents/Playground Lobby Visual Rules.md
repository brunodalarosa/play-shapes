These are the rules that should be applied for all of the playground lobby scenes in the game.

1. **Use a 2.5D gameplay composition.**  
    Build a three-dimensional environment around a single vertical gameplay plane. Characters move left/right and jump up/down. Environmental depth supplies volume and atmosphere; playable routes remain readable across the screen without requiring movement toward or away from the camera.
    
2. **Use a near-frontal, slightly elevated camera.**  
    Look almost straight at the stage, with a gentle downward tilt that reveals the tops of walkable surfaces and their front edges. Approximately 10–15 degrees below horizontal is a useful initial direction. Keep the horizon level and sideways camera rotation minimal. Favor restrained perspective with little foreshortening. Avoid an isometric view, steep overhead view, or dramatic wide-angle lens.
    
3. **Frame the whole playable arrangement.**  
    Use a wide landscape composition, preferably 16:9. Show the principal floor, elevated platforms, and open space above the highest platform for jumping. Describe distance through framing: the arena fills most of the image, its outer edges remain visible, and characters remain readable. Avoid pulling back so far that the environment overwhelms the players. Request a representative gameplay view rather than a cinematic establishing shot.
    
4. **Create a clear hierarchy of walkable levels.**  
    Establish a broad lower floor or clearly connected lower surfaces, then arrange smaller platforms at several distinct heights. Two or three upper tiers are a useful starting point when no layout has been specified. Vary platform lengths and stagger them horizontally. Preserve open space between tiers so the image communicates landing areas and possible jump paths. Avoid an arrangement of tightly stacked floors that hides characters or reads as enclosed rooms.
    
5. **Make elevated platforms belong to the setting.**  
    Give each platform a recognizable environmental identity: a shelf, awning, balcony, broad branch, projecting book, suspended tray, toy structure, or oversized household object. Use brackets, stems, ropes, adjoining architecture, or a clear whimsical suspension idea where appropriate. Keep these explanations visually simple. A platform should feel like an intentional part of the world while retaining a broad, readable landing surface.
    
6. **Keep gameplay surfaces visually unambiguous.**  
    Show continuous top surfaces, clear front edges, and enough apparent width for characters to stand. Align playable surfaces with the same gameplay plane even when their supporting objects extend into depth. Keep decorations away from landing areas and jump corridors. Background shelves, rooftops, and ledges should be less prominent or visibly separated so they do not compete with the actual platforms.
    
7. **Separate playable space from scenery.**  
    Give the playable surfaces and characters the clearest silhouettes and strongest local contrast. Use simpler shapes, quieter contrast, and fewer details behind the action. Preserve uncluttered space around players. Keep foreground objects low or near the sides so they do not obscure feet, platform edges, or jumps. Decorative depth must never make the route appear to turn into the background.
    
8. **Apply Play Shapes’ visual identity.**  
    Use simple low-poly geometry, exaggerated everyday objects, friendly toy-like proportions, bold colors, clean surfaces, soft shading, and bright ambient light. Organize the scene around a few memorable objects with generous negative space. Use selective matte or cardboard-like finishes where they suit the setting. Keep materials and details readable at gameplay distance.
    
9. **Include a consistent scale reference.**  
    For gameplay mockups, place a few small Shape Characters on different levels: simple geometric bodies with floating hands and feet. Keep their scale consistent so platform width, vertical spacing, and camera distance can be judged. An environment-only version can omit them after the composition is established. The image communicates intended spacing; actual jump reach and collision behavior need separate gameplay decisions.
    
10. **Use other games as references for composition.**  
    “Super Smash Bros.-style side-on arena framing and vertically layered platform readability” is an appropriate reference. Pair it with an explicit request for an original Play Shapes environment. Create new architecture, props, silhouettes, colors, and platform arrangements. Avoid reproducing a recognizable stage layout, franchise landmark, character, logo, or texture.
